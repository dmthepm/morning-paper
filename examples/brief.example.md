<div class="mg-kicker">Harborfront</div>
<div class="mg-title">The Lighthouse Keeper Wants a Quieter Lamp</div>
<div class="mg-dek">Forty years of faithful turning, and Port Anselm's light still works — its keeper would simply like it to hum a little less.</div>
<div class="mg-byline">By <strong>The Demo Desk</strong> · A fictional dispatch, written to show the type</div>
</div>

<p class="mg-lede">Nobody in Port Anselm remembers the lighthouse going dark. What they remember — what they cannot stop remembering, if they live within half a mile of the point — is the hum. The lamp's turning motor, installed the year the old one seized in a gale, runs a half-step flat of middle C, and on still nights it carries clear across the harbor to the bakery, the school, and the back row of the council chamber.</p>

The keeper, who has held the post longer than anyone now on the council has held anything, filed his request in writing: one replacement motor, modern, mounted on rubber. He attached a diagram. He attached, for emphasis, a recording.

<div class="mg-pull">"A lighthouse should be seen and not heard. Mine does both, and only one of them is in the job description."<span class="mg-pull-attr">The keeper, in this entirely invented interview</span></div>

The council's engineer agrees the fix is simple and the part is cheap; the argument, as ever in Port Anselm, is about everything else. Three residents have written to say the hum is the sound of the town working and ought to be protected. One has proposed tuning it properly instead of removing it. The harbormaster, asked to break the tie, said only that the fog signal was next on her list and that anyone who liked the hum was welcome to it.

The vote is scheduled for tonight, after the matter of the pier lamps. The keeper says he will accept any outcome that lets him sleep through a calm sea, and that after forty years he has earned the right to be specific about his silences. The fog, as ever, will get the last word. <span class="endmark"></span>
